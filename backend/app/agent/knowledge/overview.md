# PCOS overview

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common, long-term hormonal and metabolic condition. It can affect menstrual cycles and ovulation, skin and hair, fertility, and metabolic health. The name can be misleading: having ovarian cysts is not required for someone to have PCOS, and ovarian follicles seen on ultrasound are not the same thing as dangerous cysts. Symptoms and their impact differ from person to person. [NICHD: PCOS](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/pcos) · [ACOG: PCOS](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/polycystic-ovary-syndrome-pcos)

A screening questionnaire or risk score cannot diagnose PCOS. A clinician considers a person's history and symptoms, may examine them or order blood tests, and considers other possible explanations. No single symptom or ultrasound finding is enough by itself. [NICHD: PCOS](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/pcos) · [ACOG: PCOS](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/polycystic-ovary-syndrome-pcos)

PCOS is manageable. Care can be tailored to a person's symptoms, health priorities, and reproductive goals; it may involve lifestyle support and, when appropriate, medication or other treatments. A healthcare professional can discuss options and relevant long-term health checks. [ACOG: PCOS](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/polycystic-ovary-syndrome-pcos)

This material supports screening education only; it is not a diagnosis. Please consult a doctor about personal symptoms or decisions.
