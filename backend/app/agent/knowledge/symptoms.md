# Common PCOS symptoms

PCOS can be associated with infrequent, irregular, or absent periods because ovulation may not happen regularly. Some people notice excess coarse hair growth on the face or body, persistent acne or oily skin, or thinning hair on the scalp. Thickened, darker, velvety skin in body folds can also occur. Not everyone has the same symptoms, and these signs can have causes other than PCOS. [ACOG: PCOS](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/polycystic-ovary-syndrome-pcos) · [NICHD: PCOS](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/pcos)

Some people with PCOS have trouble becoming pregnant because ovulation is irregular, but PCOS does not mean pregnancy is impossible. PCOS is also associated with higher rates of insulin resistance and some metabolic conditions; this does not mean every person will develop them. Mood and sleep concerns can also matter and are worth discussing with a clinician. [NICHD: PCOS](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/pcos) · [CDC: PCOS and diabetes](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/risk-factors/pcos-polycystic-ovary-syndrome.html)

Symptoms alone cannot confirm PCOS. A clinician can evaluate persistent cycle changes, acne or hair changes, fertility concerns, and other possible causes. This material is for screening education, not diagnosis; please consult a doctor about your individual situation.
