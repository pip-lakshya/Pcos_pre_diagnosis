# Common PCOS myths and misconceptions

**Myth: Everyone with PCOS has ovarian cysts.** Not true. The name is misleading; ultrasound appearance is only one possible feature and is not required in every diagnosis. **Myth: You need every symptom.** Symptoms vary, and a person can have PCOS without all of them. [ACOG: five things to know about PCOS](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/experts-and-stories/the-latest/5-things-you-need-to-know-about-pcos) · [NICHD: PCOS](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/pcos)

**Myth: PCOS only affects people in larger bodies.** PCOS occurs at different body sizes. **Myth: PCOS means you cannot become pregnant.** PCOS can make ovulation and conception more difficult for some people, but pregnancy can still occur, sometimes without assistance and sometimes with treatment. A clinician can discuss options based on an individual's goals. [ACOG: five things to know about PCOS](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/experts-and-stories/the-latest/5-things-you-need-to-know-about-pcos)

**Myth: One symptom, ultrasound, or online score confirms PCOS.** Diagnosis requires professional evaluation and consideration of other explanations. **Myth: A special diet cures PCOS or everyone must avoid the same foods.** No one diet composition is established as best for all PCOS outcomes; care should be sustainable and individualized. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fertnstert.2023.07.025) · [ACOG: PCOS](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/polycystic-ovary-syndrome-pcos)

These corrections are general screening education, not a diagnosis. Please consult a doctor about your own symptoms or treatment choices.
